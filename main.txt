Lexicon Definition: The complete inventory of lexical items in a language, including words, morphemes, fixed expressions, and collocations, together with their syntactic and semantic properties. It is not merely a list but a structured set of relations governing form, meaning, and usage.

Word Formation Processes: Cover derivation (prefixation, suffixation), compounding, conversion (zero-derivation), blending, clipping, back-formation, and reduplication. Each process alters productivity and semantic transparency.

Semantic Relations: Include synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy/hypernymy, meronymy/holonymy, and collocation. These govern inference, disambiguation, and thesaurus construction.

Etymology and Semantic Change: Tracks historical borrowing, sound shifts, and meaning drift (broadening, narrowing, amelioration, pejoration). Important for disambiguation in temporal texts.

Syntax and Valency: Verbs assign thematic roles (agent, patient, instrument) to dependents. Valency frames determine permissible arguments, influencing parsing and machine translation.

Pragmatics and Contextual Meaning: Covers implicature, presupposition, speech acts, and politeness. Essential for dialogue systems and sentiment analysis.

Register and Variation: Dialect, sociolect, register (formal, technical, colloquial), and stylistic markers affect word choice and interpretation across domains.

Lexical Semantics and Sense Inventory: Polysemy, homonymy, and homographs require sense disambiguation algorithms; resources like WordNet frame structured meanings.

Morphological Analysis and Generation: Rules for inflection (tense, number, case) and derivation; critical for tokenization, lemmatization, and text normalization.

Orthography and Graphemic Systems: Spelling conventions, hyphenation, diacritics, and transliteration rules vary across languages and impact search and indexing.

Computlex and Corpus Design: Guidelines for building representative corpora; balanced coverage of genres, domains, and registers reduces sampling bias.

Lexical Resource Standards: ISO/TC37 standards, Universal Dependencies features, and annotation schemes ensure interoperability across datasets.

Language-Specific Idiosyncrasies: Articles, classifiers, serial verb constructions, and polysynthetic morphology demand language-specific modeling strategies.

Neologism Detection and Adoption: Metrics include frequency trajectory, morphological transparency, and social diffusion; early detection supports dynamic lexicon updates.

Evaluation Metrics for Lexicons: Coverage, ambiguity resolution rate, recall on unseen words, and alignment with gold-standard annotations quantify lexicon quality.
